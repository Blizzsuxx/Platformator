# Performance and memory analysis

The matched Release matrix completed 68 jobs with no capture failures. Each of the 36 workload/population/worker cells has five independent processes per version; `timings/comparison.csv` reports complete-step, broad-phase, narrow-phase and cleanup averages, mean per-process p95, between-process standard deviation, and observed candidate/contact counts. The retained archive contains the raw samples and schedule. The reconstructed baseline passed the original controlled invariant comparisons, but the original full source-manifest fingerprint could not be rechecked after temporary-directory loss.

For the targeted 1,000-pair churn fixture, baseline/candidate complete-step averages were 4.026/4.119 ms at one worker and 1.245/1.240 ms at six workers; mean p95 values were 5.358/5.772 ms and 1.595/1.633 ms respectively. Corresponding between-process standard deviations were 0.228/0.295 ms and 0.009/0.032 ms. Average broad-phase times were 3.748/3.812 ms and 1.113/1.112 ms. Both versions averaged 500 candidates and 500 contacts over the alternating overlap cycle, and the separately validated enter/exit traces match. Complete-step one-to-six-worker ratios were approximately 3.24× baseline and 3.32× candidate. These differences are small relative to the observed variation and do not establish a speedup or regression caused by slot removal.

The primary 1,000-pair persistent-contact run showed candidate complete-step averages 3.3% and 4.7% higher at one and six workers (0.0819/0.0846 ms and 0.0796/0.0834 ms baseline/candidate). A separate alternating five-pair follow-up with the same two scripted endpoints gave 0.0817/0.0825 ms at one worker and 0.0854/0.0832 ms at six workers; per-run variation and the reversal at six workers make the initial difference inconclusive. Its raw captures are retained separately. Several spatial and object-churn groups had zero candidate pairs, so their differences cannot be attributed to pair-slot work. Six-worker unconstrained rigid-body runs had different average contact counts between versions and are not a controlled like-for-like timing comparison.

Object sizes fell on this build by 8 bytes each for `ColliderPair`, `Audio`, and `GameObject`, and by 4 bytes for `ClipPointWithData`. An instrumented baseline copy observed 1,024 retained elements in each removed pair vector at a 1,000-pair peak: 16 KiB of element storage together, excluding overhead. One process-wide RSS sample per configuration was noisy and did not show a reduction. Details, instrumentation boundaries and raw data are in `work-memory.md`. No universal runtime or RSS improvement is claimed.
