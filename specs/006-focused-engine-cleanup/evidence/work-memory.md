# Partial work and memory measurements

A separate LD_PRELOAD correctness run interposed `stat`, `lstat`, `readlink` and `realpath` in the common Release texture probe. Counts include process/runtime startup, one initial load and either 1 or 100 repeated requests; compare each tree's two counts rather than the aggregate as a per-request count. Baseline 1/100: stat+lstat 6/204, realpath 3/102, readlink 2/2. Candidate 1/100: stat+lstat 4/4, realpath 2/2, readlink 2/2. This observes no additional intercepted calls for 99 extra candidate hits, versus 198 stat/lstat and 99 realpath calls in baseline. Interposition does not prove absence of every possible filesystem syscall. Source and raw logs are in `work/`; the non-instrumented timings are separate.

The candidate's aliases store a raw request and canonical string plus reverse-list entries; exact retained bytes, allocation counts, debug-object copies/moves and measured eviction costs are not established by this fixture. Source-level eviction work is proportional to aliases for the resource. The standalone type check in `us3-validation.md` establishes nothrow move constructibility but does not count moves. `perf trace` could not access host tracing; see `coverage.md`.

A separate instrumented vector-growth work fixture linked against each debug build created 1,000 objects with 16 vertices and an 80-character name. Across 11 growths, the baseline replaced all 1,023 prior vertex buffers; the candidate preserved all 1,023, consistent with moving rather than copying them. The fixture compares owned vertex buffer addresses before/after growth and is not a timing run. It does not measure total allocator calls or rendered output. Source, compiler driver and raw logs are in `work/`.

A separate glibc allocation-interposition run counted process-wide malloc/calloc/realloc calls for 1 versus 100 repeated texture requests. Baseline: 1,108 versus 4,375 calls (difference 3,267); candidate: 1,077 versus 1,176 (difference 99). This includes runtime startup and caller-side string construction and is a single run per cell; it is not a precise retained-memory attribution or timing measure. Source and logs are retained in `work/`. Preliminary ten- and hundred-load/eviction timings are in `asset-preliminary.md`; they show the candidate's churn control slower in those small samples, requiring broader measurement before a general conclusion.

A separate glibc `mallinfo2().uordblks` process-level probe held one texture and resolved 300 valid distinct slash spellings. Baseline allocated bytes rose from 309,984 to 321,808 (+11,824); candidate rose from 309,712 to 502,320 (+192,608), an approximately 180,784-byte differential while these unusually long aliases were live. After release the respective totals were 311,216 and 366,432. These are single-process totals, include allocator and library activity, depend on path lengths, and do not precisely isolate alias storage or prove a leak; alias maps are behaviorally checked to invalidate on release. Source and logs are retained in `work/`. The prior fixture's one-/hundred-operation churn timings include load and final-release work, rather than isolating eviction alone.

The debug-growth and 300-alias memory fixtures were rebuilt and repeated after the final internal PathManager signature cleanup; they produced the same recorded counts and byte totals. The source fixtures, logs and method above correspond to that final version.

After the final internal PathManager signature cleanup, the interposition checks were repeated using the warmed common fixture. For 1 versus 100 measured hits, baseline stat/lstat counts were 206/404 and realpath counts 103/202, while candidate counts stayed at 4/4 and 2/2 respectively; the additional 100 warmup hits explain the larger baseline totals. Allocation counts were 4,408/7,675 baseline and 1,177/1,276 candidate, again differences of 3,267 and 99. Final-version logs are `work/final-fs-*` and `work/final-alloc-*`.

## Phase 7: exception safety and capacity reclamation

A 300-canonical-entry test grew both alias maps to 541 buckets. After 299 resources were evicted, each retained 31 buckets and the remaining alias remained valid; the test also forced an allocation failure during a shrink, verified removal stayed nonthrowing, and confirmed later shrink/reuse. Small tables (at most 64 buckets) are retained to avoid reallocating on every ordinary load/release. Larger tables shrink geometrically when below one-quarter occupancy; if reclamation allocation fails, the old capacity is retained and a later removal can retry. The state bound therefore has a documented best-effort exception under memory pressure.

The separate 300-alias process-level `mallinfo2` probe measured prior-feature initial/live/released bytes of 308,528/501,136/365,248 and final candidate 309,712/502,320/362,096; the pre-feature baseline was 309,984/321,808/311,216. These single-process totals include allocator and library effects and do not precisely attribute remaining bytes; they are not proof of a leak or exact map size. The direct bucket-count test above establishes the container behavior. Raw logs and the unchanged fixture are in `evidence/convergence/`.

A separate direct 300-key eviction fixture compared the prior and final alias helpers over 50 timed fill/remove cycles per process, after five warmup cycles, with ten shuffled independent processes each on CPU 0. Mean totals were 1.673 ± 0.062 ms prior and 1.799 ± 0.108 ms final, roughly 7.5% higher in this isolated high-alias-count control; the timed scope covers removal only. Both implementations used the same fixture and Release compiler settings. Sources and individual samples are in `evidence/convergence/alias-eviction.cpp`, `006-prior-aliases.h` and `alias-eviction.json`.

In a separate process-wide allocation-interposition check, increasing repeated hits from 1 to 100 added 3,267 allocation calls in the pre-feature baseline and 99 in both prior and final candidates. This includes caller-side string allocation and does not establish exact cache allocation counts; raw results are in `evidence/convergence/006-convergence-alloc-*.txt`.
